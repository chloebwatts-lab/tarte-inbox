# The Job Map

A 20 minute format for Savannah, Georgia and the two new supervisors to say clearly what their job actually is, what they want more of, and what should move to someone else.

Written for Tarte management. Internal only, never customer facing.

---

## Why it works this way round

Job descriptions written from a blank page are slow, vague, and out of date within a month. This does the opposite. People list the real work first, tag every line with two letters, and the description falls out of the tags.

It gives you three things from one sitting:

1. A clear role description for each manager, in their own words.
2. A ranked list of what to hand to the new supervisors, built from work that already exists.
3. A development conversation, because "what I enjoy" is captured in the same pass as "what I do."

---

## Step 1. The brain dump (10 minutes)

Write every task you do. One short line each. Do not organise it, do not rank it, do not be polite about it. Aim for 25 to 40 lines.

Prompts if you stall: what do you do before service, during service, after service, once a week, once a month, and what do people interrupt you for.

## Step 2. Two letters per line (5 minutes)

**Energy. How you feel doing it:**

- `L` = Love it. Time disappears.
- `O` = Okay. Don't mind it either way.
- `D` = Drain. You put it off, or it wrecks the day.

**Handover. Could someone else do this:**

- `M` = Only me. Needs my judgement, my relationships, or my authority.
- `T` = Teachable. Someone could learn it in two or three shifts alongside me.
- `A` = Anyone. It is a checklist. Show once and go.

Add rough time and how often. Guesses are fine, do not go and measure.

```
Fortnightly roster            D T    2 hrs, every second Sunday
Function site visits          L M    45 min, 2 to 3 a week
Chasing supplier credits      D A    20 min, most days
```

## Step 3. The four piles (5 minutes)

The two letters sort themselves:

|  | **M** (only me) | **T / A** (teachable or anyone) |
|---|---|---|
| **L / O** | **PROTECT.** This is your real job. | **TRADE.** Keep one or two as breathing room. Hand the rest over as training. |
| **D** | **FIX.** Cannot be handed over, so change how it is done, time box it, or automate it. | **GIVE AWAY FIRST.** This is the supervisor list. |

Sort the "give away first" box by hours per month, biggest first. That list is where the two supervisor roles come from. You are not inventing jobs, you are collecting real work that is currently being done by the wrong person at the wrong pay grade.

## Step 4. Three sentences

1. **More of:** ...
2. **Off my plate first:** ...
3. **Want to learn this quarter:** ...

Sentence 1 and 3 are the bit most places skip. They are what turns this from a delegation exercise into a reason to stay.

## Step 5. The role description writes itself

> **Role description** = the PROTECT pile + the FIX pile, written as plain sentences.
> **Development plan** = the three sentences from Step 4.

Nothing else. No competency framework, no five page position description.

---

## Handing a task over: the five line card

A task does not move until these five lines are written down. Two minutes each.

1. **The task:**
2. **Done looks like:**
3. **When:**
4. **You decide / you ask me:**
5. **Stage:**

**Line 4 is the one that stops handovers going wrong.** Be specific with numbers.
Example: "You decide table moves and anything comped up to $20. You ask me before refunding a deposit, changing a function price, or replying to a bad review."

**Line 5 is what stops delegating from meaning dumping.** Four stages, move one stage a week:

`1. Watch me` → `2. Do it with me` → `3. Do it, tell me after` → `4. It is yours`

If a task slips back a stage twice, it goes back in the FIX pile. The process is the problem, not the person.

---

## Cadence

- **Fortnightly for the first month** while the supervisors settle in. Fifteen minutes, and only line 5 of each card gets discussed.
- **Then quarterly**, full Job Map redo.
- **One standing rule:** nothing gets added to anyone's map without something coming off it.

## Worked example (Georgia, illustrative only)

**Brain dump, tagged:**

```
Answering hello@ enquiries          O T   60 min daily
Function quotes and packages        L M   3 to 4 a week
Chasing deposit payments            D A   15 min daily
Fortnightly roster                  D T   2 hrs
Ordering and stock counts           D A   45 min, twice weekly
Training new floor staff            L T   ad hoc
Supplier credits and invoice errors D A   20 min most days
Bad review replies                  D M   2 a week
Menu and specials with kitchen      L M   weekly
Opening and closing checks          O A   daily
```

**Sorts to:**

- **PROTECT:** function quotes, menu and specials, review replies sit here only because they need her authority.
- **FIX:** review replies. She hates them and cannot hand them over, so the fix is a template plus a rule that she only writes the first two lines.
- **GIVE AWAY FIRST (ranked by hours):** ordering and stock counts (90 min a week), deposit chasing (75 min a week), supplier credits (100 min a week), roster (2 hrs a fortnight).
- **TRADE:** training new floor staff. She loves it and it is teachable, so she keeps it deliberately.

**Supervisor role, assembled from the give away list:** stock and ordering, supplier credits, deposit chasing, and first draft of the roster. That is a real, coherent job, and nobody had to invent it.

**Three sentences:** more of function work and menu development, deposit chasing off first, wants to learn costing and margins this quarter.
